My Approach to Product

I believe good product management sits at the intersection of curiosity, clarity, and execution.

Understanding what people actually need. Questioning assumptions before jumping to solutions. Using data without losing sight of human behaviour. And turning ambiguity into decisions that move products forward.

Each of these took a problem from first principles through to a working solution — research, strategy, design, and a prototype to test it against.

01 — Vision+

What happens when a purchase only happens once every two years?

The challenge

Lenskart's business leans heavily on a single high-value transaction, with 12–24 months typically passing before a customer returns — a structural gap between purchase moments and any ongoing relationship.

What I explored

I interviewed 30 users, mapped the competitive landscape, and designed Vision+ — a concept that turns static prescription data into an ongoing engagement loop, supported by a phased go-to-market plan and a working prototype.

02 — Tap & Recharge

Why do people default to the same app, every single time?

The challenge

Paytm competes for mobile recharges against Google Pay's default-app advantage and operator apps' plan accuracy — a habit that reminders and cashback alone don't shift.

What I explored

I segmented recharge behaviour by city tier and platform, benchmarked competitors, and used RICE to prioritize a one-tap concept built around habit-loop design, then prototyped it end to end.

Process

How I Think Through a Product Problem

01

Start With Why

Before discussing features, understand the underlying problem and the behaviour behind it.

02

Find the Signal

Research, customer conversations, data, and competitive insights each reveal different parts of the story.

03

Explore Before Committing

The first solution is rarely the best one. Explore alternatives before narrowing down.

04

Prioritise Ruthlessly

Not everything deserves to be built. Focus on what creates meaningful value for users and the business.

05

Ship, Learn, Repeat

Every product decision is a hypothesis. The real learning continues after execution.
